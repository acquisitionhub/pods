Trenching and armoured cabling from the site's incoming supply to each pod. Cost depends heavily on distance. If the landowner has an existing supply near the site: £800–£2,000 per pod. £3,500
Clean water: connection to mains (cheapest if nearby) or a holding tank/borehole. Composting toilets (£800–£1,500) are cheapest but limit luxury appeal. Environment Agency permit required for discharge.

Landowner Outreach Strategy
How to find, approach, and close a revenue share deal with a Chilterns landowner — without buying land.

The Revenue Share Deal Structure

You bring
Capital, pods, operations & bookings
Landowner brings
Land, planning support & local credibility
Landowner earns
15–25% of gross revenue, monthly
Typical term
3–5 year rolling agreement
Pod ownership
You own the pods — remove at end
Ideal land type
Unused paddock, woodland edge, farm field

Where to find landowners

  • Farmers Weekly online classifieds — "farm diversification" section
  • Glampitect's land matching service (connect.glampitect.com)
  • Facebook Groups: "Chilterns Farmers", "Bucks Countryside", local parish groups
  • Land Registry — identify field owners by postcode in target areas (£3/title search)
  • Rural estate agents: Strutt & Parker, Savills Rural, Brown & Co
  • Country Land & Business Association (CLA) regional events
  • Cold walk-ins to equestrian centres with unused paddocks
  • Pub landlords with large unused beer gardens or field attachments

Target land profile

  • Minimum 0.5–1 acre for 3 pods with safe spacing
  • Screened from main roads — key for AONB planning approval
  • Within or adjacent to Chilterns AONB boundary
  • Valley or woodland edge setting — strongest planning case
  • Within 15 min walk of a footpath or trail (guest appeal)
  • Access to electricity and water supply nearby
  • Good road access for guest arrivals and pod delivery
  • Areas: Wendover, Tring, Great Missenden, Princes Risborough

What landowners worry about

  • Liability if a guest is injured on their land
  • Planning permission risk — who pays if refused?
  • What happens to the pods if the deal ends
  • Noise and nuisance from guests affecting neighbours
  • Tax implications of receiving revenue (HMRC)
  • Whether you'll actually manage it professionally

How to address their concerns

  • You carry public liability insurance (minimum £5m) — show the policy
  • You pay for planning application costs upfront — their risk is zero
  • Pods are removable structures — clearly stated in the agreement
  • Minimum stay = 2 nights, adults-only option reduces noise risk
  • Suggest they take independent tax advice — don't advise them yourself
  • Offer references or a trial 28-day permitted development period

Outreach sequence — what to do week by week

  • Week 1: Use MAGIC (magic.defra.gov.uk) and Land Registry to identify 20 land parcels in Wendover/Tring/Great Missenden areas — note owner names and addresses
  • Week 2: Post personalised letters to all 20 landowners. Simultaneously post in 3–4 Chilterns/Bucks farming Facebook groups
  • Week 3: Follow up any early responders; approach equestrian centres directly by phone or email (5 targets per week)
  • Week 4: Attend one CLA or rural business event in the region — face-to-face is far more effective than cold outreach
  • Ongoing: Register on Glampitect's land matching service; keep posting in local groups with your specific pitch
Chilterns AONB Planning Checklist
What you need to do — and in what order — to get planning permission for a pod site in the Chilterns.
1

Confirm land is outside the tightest AONB core or find AONB-adjacent land

Use MAGIC (magic.defra.gov.uk) to check the exact AONB boundary. Land just outside the AONB boundary still benefits from scenic setting but faces standard planning rules — much easier for a first site.

2

Engage a local planning consultant before submitting anything

Someone with Chilterns or Bucks planning experience is essential. Budget £1,500–£3,000 for pre-application advice. This single step dramatically increases your approval chances and saves money long-term.

3

Submit a pre-application enquiry to the local planning authority

Before a full application, pay for a pre-app enquiry (£300–£600). You get officer feedback on whether a pod site is likely to be accepted in principle — before you spend serious money.

4

Commission an ecological survey if near woodland or hedgerows

Required in most AONB applications. A Phase 1 Habitat Survey costs £600–£1,200. If bats are present, a Phase 2 bat survey is needed (£1,500–£2,500) — allow for this in your budget.

5

Use low-profile, AONB-compliant pod designs

Pods must be under 3.5–4m in height. Curved or arched rooflines that blend into the landscape perform best with AONB planning officers. Avoid flat-roofed or metal-clad structures in this landscape.

6

Demonstrate rural diversification benefit in your planning statement

NPPF supports sustainable tourism in rural areas. Your statement should show economic benefit to the local area, use of local suppliers, and low landscape impact — this is what tilts planning officers in your favour.

7

Propose natural screening — hedging, tree planting, or existing cover

A landscaping plan showing how pods will be screened from roads and neighbouring properties dramatically strengthens an AONB application. Even proposing to plant a native hedgerow counts in your favour.

8

Plan your drainage and waste water from day one

Each pod needs a legal waste water solution. Options: connection to mains sewer, package treatment plant (~£2,000–£5,000 per unit), or composting toilet systems. Must be included in your planning application.

9

Consider applying for just 2 pods initially, with option to extend

Starting with 2 pods is often easier to get approved in an AONB. Once established and trading, applying for a third pod extension is treated more favourably — you have a track record of responsible operation.

10

Get a Land Registry title plan for the site before any application

You need proof of site ownership (or landowner consent) to submit a planning application. A signed letter of consent from the landowner is sufficient if you don't own the land. Get this agreed and documented early.

Key contacts for the Chilterns: Chiltern District Council, South Bucks District Council, Dacorum Borough Council (for Herts edge), Chilterns Conservation Board (chilternsaonb.org) — their pre-application guidance is publicly available and worth reading before you spend anything.
