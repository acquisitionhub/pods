Week 1

Build your target list

Research, map, and identify 20 specific land parcels with owner names and addresses. This is your prospect database.

Goal by end of week: 20 named landowners with postal addresses, ready to write to.
✓
Open MAGIC and map the Chilterns AONB boundary
Go to magic.defra.gov.uk → click "Add a layer" → search "AONB" → toggle on the Chilterns AONB layer. Note exactly where the boundary runs around Wendover, Tring, Great Missenden, Princes Risborough, and Chinnor. Screenshot and save it. Land just inside or just outside this line is your primary target zone.
magic.defra.gov.uk 30 mins Free Do first
✓
Use Land Registry map to identify 30 target parcels
Go to search.land-registry.service.gov.uk → switch to map view → zoom into your target areas. Click on unused-looking fields, paddocks, woodland edges, and old orchards adjacent to farmhouses. Note the title number for each one. You want 30 parcels to end up with 20 usable contacts (some will be companies, trusts, or hard to reach).
land-registry.service.gov.uk 2 hours Free to browse
✓
Buy title registers for your 30 target parcels
For each title number, pay £3 to download the title register. This gives you the registered owner's name and their correspondence address. Do all 30 in one session — it takes about an hour and costs around £90. Filter out any that are large corporations, councils, or National Trust — focus on individual names and family trusts.
Land Registry 1 hour ~£90 (30 × £3) Core spend
✓
Cross-reference names on 192.com and BT Phone Book
For each name, search 192.com to confirm their current address and find a phone number where possible. BT Phone Book (bt.com/directory-enquiries) is free for landlines. Note any phone numbers — a call is always more effective than a letter. Build a simple spreadsheet: Name | Address | Phone | Land parcel | Notes.
192.com / BT Phone Book 1–2 hours £0–£20
✓
Search each name on LinkedIn and Facebook
For each landowner, do a quick LinkedIn and Facebook search. You're not connecting yet — just checking if they're findable online and noting their profession or interests. Estate managers, equestrian operators, and younger farm owners are often on LinkedIn. Note anyone with "diversification", "tourism", or "rural business" in their profile — these are warm leads.
LinkedIn / Facebook 45 mins Free
✓
Join 5 Chilterns Facebook groups
Request to join: Chilterns Farming & Countryside · Buckinghamshire Farming Network · Aylesbury Vale Countryside · Great Missenden Community · Wendover Community Notice Board. Don't post yet — just get approved and observe for a few days to understand the tone and who's active.
Facebook 15 mins Free
Week 2

Send the letters & go live online

Post your personalised letters to all 20 landowners. Launch your Facebook presence simultaneously.

Goal by end of week: 20 letters posted, Facebook posts live in 3+ groups, Glampitect listing created.
✓
Write and print 20 personalised letters
Use the postal letter template from the Chilterns Toolkit as your base. Personalise each one — mention the specific village or area near their land ("I've been looking at land in the Wendover area"), and if you found their name from Land Registry don't reference it directly (just say "I'm reaching out to local landowners in the area"). Print on plain A4, hand-sign each one.
2 hours ~£10 (postage + printing) High impact
✓
Post all 20 letters first class
Send first class — it matters. Second class feels like junk mail. Hand-write the envelope address for each one rather than using a printed label. A handwritten envelope gets opened. Budget around £15 for stamps. Expect 2–4 responses from 20 letters — that's a normal response rate for this kind of cold outreach.
30 mins ~£15
✓
Post your Facebook outreach message in 3 groups
Use the script below as your base. Post in: Chilterns Farming & Countryside, Buckinghamshire Farming Network, and one local village group. Space posts 2–3 days apart — don't post in all groups on the same day. Monitor comments and reply promptly to any interest.
Facebook 20 mins Free
📣 Facebook post script
Hello — I'm Julien, based in Milton Keynes, and I'm looking to partner with a landowner in the Chilterns area. I'm developing a small glamping pod business and looking for a section of unused land — a paddock, old orchard, woodland edge, or quiet field — to develop 2–3 premium pods. Here's the deal: I fund everything and handle all operations, planning costs, and guest management. You provide the land and receive a monthly income from revenue — typically £500–£1,200/month for a 3-pod site with no upfront cost to you at all. The pods are low-profile, AONB-friendly structures. If planning is refused for any reason, the pods never arrive and your land is untouched. If you have suitable land or know someone who might — I'd love a chat. No obligation, nothing to sign. DM me or drop a comment below. Thank you 🙏
✓
Register on Glampitect's land matching service
Go to connect.glampitect.com and create a profile as a glamping operator looking for land. This is a passive lead generator — landowners who are actively looking to diversify are already on the platform. Fill out your profile fully, specify Chilterns / Bucks / Herts as your target area, and describe the revenue share deal clearly.
connect.glampitect.com 30 mins Free
✓
Contact 5 parish councils by email
Find the parish clerk's contact for: Wendover, Great Missenden, Chinnor, Tring, and Princes Risborough. Email each clerk asking if they can include a short notice in their next newsletter or circulate to members. Write 2–3 sentences max — "local entrepreneur seeking land partnership for glamping project, happy to discuss with any interested landowners." Most will say yes for free.
45 mins Free
Week 3

Hit the equestrian centres

Direct phone and email outreach to livery yards and riding centres with unused paddocks — your highest-conversion target type.

Goal by end of week: 10 equestrian centres contacted, 2–3 conversations started.
✓
Build a list of 15 equestrian centres in target areas
Search Google Maps for "livery yard Wendover", "riding school Tring", "equestrian centre Great Missenden", "livery yard Princes Risborough". Also check equinebusinessdirectory.com filtered to Buckinghamshire and Hertfordshire. For each, note: name, phone, email, and whether they appear to have paddocks beyond their main yard on Google Maps/Street View.
Google Maps / equinebusinessdirectory.com 1 hour Free
✓
Call 10 equestrian centres — use the phone script below
Call between 9–11am Tuesday to Thursday — best time to reach yard owners. Ask for the owner by name if you have it. If they're not available, ask when is a good time to call back — don't leave a message on the first attempt. Aim to have a conversation, not just deliver a pitch. Ask questions: "Do you have any paddocks that aren't currently in use?"
2 hours over 2–3 days Free Highest conversion
📞 Phone script
"Hi, my name's Julien Kabongo — I'm a local entrepreneur based in Milton Keynes. I wonder if I could speak to the owner for just two minutes? [If owner available:] Thanks for taking the call. I'll be brief — I'm developing a glamping pod business in the Chilterns and I'm looking to partner with landowners who have unused paddock space. The idea is that I fund everything — the pods, planning, operations — and pay the landowner a monthly income from bookings. No cost or hassle on your side at all. I just wanted to ask — do you have any paddocks or fields that aren't currently being used? Even a small section of half an acre or more could work well. [If yes or curious:] That's great to hear. Would you be open to a quick meeting so I can show you the numbers? I'm flexible on timing and happy to come to you."
✓
Email the remaining 5 equestrian centres
For any centres you couldn't reach by phone, send the email template from the Chilterns Toolkit, personalised with the centre name and a specific mention of their location ("I noticed you're based near the Ridgeway — the setting would be ideal"). Follow up by phone 3 days later if no response.
45 mins Free
✓
Follow up any letter responses received this week
Letters sent in Week 2 should start generating responses by now. Reply same day to any contact. Your first response should be warm, brief, and move toward a phone call or in-person meeting — not a long email. Offer 2–3 specific times you're free to talk.

Week 4

Drive the area + pub visits

Ground-level reconnaissance across your target areas. The best sites are found in person, not online.

Goal by end of week: 2 Saturday drives done, 5+ additional sites identified, 3 pubs or rural businesses visited.
✓
Saturday drive #1 — Wendover to Great Missenden loop
Drive the B4009 and surrounding lanes. Look for: paddocks with no animals, old orchards, scrubby fields adjacent to farmhouses, woodland edge parcels with good screening. For any promising site, note the postcode and photograph the gate or any name boards. When you get home, run postcodes through Land Registry map view. Allow 3–4 hours including stops.
Saturday morning, 3–4 hrs Petrol Do this
✓
Saturday drive #2 — Tring to Princes Risborough loop
Cover the A41 corridor and Ivinghoe area. The Vale of Aylesbury fringe just outside the tightest AONB zone often has good rural land with easier planning. Same approach — note postcodes, photograph gates, look for unused paddocks. Pay attention to any farms with "diversification" or holiday accommodation signage — they've already thought about this and may want to expand.
Saturday morning, 3–4 hrs Petrol
✓
Visit 3 rural pubs with large gardens or attached fields
Go in, have a drink, and ask to speak to the landlord. Frame it as: "I'm looking at developing some glamping in the area and wondered if you'd ever thought about it — you've got a lovely bit of land here." Pub landlords think about income diversification constantly. Even if they're not the landowner, they know who owns the field behind them. This is intelligence gathering as much as outreach.
1 afternoon Cost of a pint Underrated
✓
Run new postcodes from drives through Land Registry
For any new parcels identified on the Saturday drives, buy the title registers (£3 each) and add the owners to your prospect spreadsheet. Write to any new names using the same personalised letter approach. This is the compounding loop — every drive generates new leads.
Land Registry 1 hour £3/parcel
Week 5

Estate agents & CLA

Activate professional networks. One warm introduction from a rural agent or CLA contact is worth 20 cold letters.

Goal by end of week: 4 rural agents briefed, CLA event booked, 1 in-person meeting with a promising landowner.
✓
Call the rural land departments at 4 estate agents
Call: Savills Rural (Beaconsfield office), Strutt & Parker (Oxford office), Carter Jonas (Oxford or Aylesbury), Brown & Co (regional). Ask for the rural land surveyor or farm agent. Explain you're a glamping operator looking for a revenue share partnership on unused land — do they have any clients who might be interested in a conversation? You're not asking them to broker a sale, just to pass your name on.
1 hour Free High value
✓
Find and book the next CLA Thames Valley regional event
Go to cla.org.uk/events and filter by Thames Valley / South East region. The CLA holds farm walks, rural business seminars, and networking breakfasts — all attended by exactly the landowners you're looking for. Book as an associate or guest member. If the next event is more than 4 weeks away, email the regional office and ask if they have any informal meetings or introductions they could facilitate.
cla.org.uk 30 mins to research + book £0–£50 event fee
✓
Prepare a one-page landowner proposition document
A single A4 page (or clean PDF) that you can email or hand to anyone who asks "can you send me something?" It should cover: what you're proposing, what the landowner gets (monthly income, no cost, no management), how the revenue share works, typical earnings at different occupancy levels, and your contact details. Keep it clean and professional — no clip art, no jargon.
2–3 hours to write and design Free Essential
✓
Hold your first in-person meeting with any interested landowner
If you have any responses from Weeks 2–4, this is the week to meet in person — always at their property. Walk the land. Ask questions. Listen more than you talk. The goal of this first meeting is not to close a deal — it's to understand their situation, build trust, and see whether the land is physically suitable. Bring your one-pager and the financial model on your phone to show indicative earnings.
1–2 hours per meeting Petrol Priority
Week 6

Review, recontact & keep the pipeline warm

Review what's worked, follow up every open thread, and set up the ongoing rhythm that keeps leads flowing.

Goal by end of week: Pipeline reviewed, all warm leads followed up, ongoing monthly rhythm established.
✓
Follow up every contact who hasn't responded yet
Go back through your spreadsheet. Anyone who received a letter and hasn't replied — send one follow-up letter or call if you have a number. Keep it short: "I wrote to you a few weeks ago about a potential land partnership. I just wanted to make sure my letter reached you and see if it sparked any interest." Most deals come from the second or third contact, not the first.
2 hours ~£5 postage
✓
Attend a CLA event or rural business networking breakfast
Introduce yourself to at least 5 people. Your pitch in person: "I help landowners generate income from unused land — I develop glamping sites on a revenue share basis, I fund everything, and the landowner just collects monthly payments." Have your one-pager in your jacket pocket. Don't hard-sell — ask questions, be curious about their land situation.
Half day High value
✓
Post a second round of Facebook outreach in new groups
Now post in the groups you haven't used yet — Aylesbury Vale Countryside, Hertfordshire Rural Business Network, and 1–2 local village groups. Slightly vary the wording from the first post — reference the progress you've made ("I've been meeting some lovely landowners across the Chilterns over the past few weeks and I'm still looking for one more site...").
Facebook 30 mins Free
✓
Set your monthly ongoing rhythm
After Week 6, this becomes a steady monthly habit rather than an intensive sprint. Schedule: 1 Saturday drive per month · 1 new Facebook post per month · 5 new Land Registry searches per month · Check Glampitect dashboard weekly · Follow up any warm leads within 48 hours. The right landowner might appear in month 3 or month 6 — consistency wins.
Ongoing — ~4 hrs/month ~£15/month

Ongoing — every week

These don't have a week — they run continuously from Week 1 onwards.

📬 Reply same day
Any response to a letter, email, Facebook message, or call — reply the same day. Speed signals professionalism and prevents the landowner going cold.
📊 Update your spreadsheet
After every contact, note the date, what was said, and the next action. A prospect who said "not now" in Week 2 might be a yes in Week 10.
🔍 Monitor Glampitect weekly
Check your dashboard on connect.glampitect.com every Monday. New land listings appear regularly — set an alert if the platform supports it.
🤝 Ask for referrals
Any landowner who says no — ask if they know anyone else who might be interested. Rural communities talk. One warm referral beats 10 cold letters.
